Real-life Digital Marketing Case Study

How Gully Labs rewrote
India's premium brand playbook

Delhi NCR · Founded August 2023 · ₹30 Cr Series A (Jan 2026)

Gully Labs didn't build a sneaker brand. They built a cultural movement — and used community engagement, content-first SMO, organic SEO, and GA4-led decisions to take a zero-follower Instagram page to India's most-talked-about homegrown sneaker brand.

02 — Strategy

How they built the playbook

Before launch · 6 months of community building
Community before product  SME SMO

The founders spent 6 months building an audience before making a single pair. They wrote about why India needed its own desi sneaker brand and actively solicited feedback on what people wanted to wear. Product direction was shaped by community input — before manufacturing began.

Content-first Instagram strategy SMO

Started as a page called "Badass Indians" — celebrating real, under-the-radar Indian stories. Built narrative gravity before any product existed. Profile optimised around cultural keywords, not just footwear terms.

Community feedback loops SME

Founders posted written manifestos, asked questions, and ran informal polls with early followers: what Indian design elements would you wear? What stories should sneakers tell? First buyers became co-creators.

Manifesto & storytelling SEO SEO

Long-form content on why India was invisible in global sneaker culture indexed early on Google. Cultural keyword targeting around "Indian streetwear," "desi sneaker brand" and "Indian design footwear" built organic reach before launch.

Pre-launch interest tracking GA4

GA4 installed on a pre-launch landing page. Events tracked: email sign-up, "Notify Me" clicks, referral source by Instagram post. Gave data-backed evidence of demand before production decisions were finalised.

Launch · Diwali drop framing
Don't launch a product — tell a story  SME SMO

The debut collection wasn't positioned as a product launch. It was framed as a Diwali story. Every design element, every post, every caption referenced Indian culture specifically — not Western aesthetics at a lower price point. The Vogue feature and a viral Reel both came within the first few months.

Cultural narrative Reels SMO

Reels structured as stories, not product demos: the craft of the karigars, the symbolism behind design motifs, the meaning of the word "Gully." One Reel went viral, driving a significant spike in D2C traffic, tracked via GA4 UTM links.

Vogue India feature SME

Landed a Vogue India feature in the first few months — not through paid placement but through a genuine pitch on cultural authenticity. Community reshared it widely, generating thousands of tagged mentions and organic UGC.

Drop pages as editorial content SEO

Each product drop had a dedicated story page — not just a product listing. The Diwali launch page included cultural context, design notes and karigar profiles. These pages ranked for long-tail cultural keywords months after the drop.

Founder-led personal brand SME

Arjun's personal Instagram and LinkedIn were used to share the founder journey authentically. Posts about the six months of research, failed prototypes and community conversations drove massive DM volume and press inbound from Inc42, GQ and others.

Growth · Collabs & cultural drops
Strategic collaborations as earned media  SME SEO

Gully Labs pursued collabs with culturally credible partners — not just brands with big budgets. Royal Enfield, CMF by Nothing (Nothing's consumer brand), and the Tata Sierra comeback all chose Gully Labs specifically because of shared values around Indian design identity. Each collaboration created its own content cycle.

Royal Enfield collab drop SME

A limited sneaker collab with Royal Enfield — tying motorcycling heritage to street culture. The drop sold out, the video content reached both sneaker and moto communities, and the story became a PR piece covered by multiple publications.

Hockey drop — under-told stories SME

Released a sneaker honoring India's two Olympic Hockey medals — athletes who got minimal mainstream coverage. The launch video (2 mins 4 seconds) was described as "pure emotion." Community shared it widely without any paid amplification.

Collab-driven backlinks SEO

Each high-profile collab (Royal Enfield, Tata Sierra, CMF by Nothing, Bombay Sweet Shop) generated press coverage from Indian Retailer, Inc42, GQ, Vogue, Fibre2Fashion and others — creating a network of high-authority backlinks to gullylabs.com.

Per-drop GA4 funnels GA4

Each collab drop had its own GA4 event stream: content_view → product_page → add_to_cart → purchase. Drop-specific UTM parameters tracked which media coverage, Instagram Reel, or creator post drove actual conversions versus only awareness.

Scale · Omnichannel + Shark Tank + Series A
From D2C to omnichannel — data-led expansion  GA4 SEO

After validating the D2C model, Gully Labs used GA4 data on customer geography and acquisition costs to decide where to open offline. Shark Tank India Season 5 gave them a national TV audience — ₹1 Cr from Aman Gupta at a ₹175 Cr valuation — followed weeks later by a ₹30 Cr Series A.

GA4-guided offline expansion GA4

GA4 audience reports showed highest repeat purchase rates from Delhi NCR, Bengaluru and Hyderabad. Offline footprint followed the data: Panchsheel Enclave flagship (Delhi), Extra Butter (Mumbai), Broadway (Hyderabad).

Myntra + AJIO SEO listings SEO

Product listings on Myntra and AJIO optimised with cultural keywords and story-led descriptions — not generic spec sheets. "Indian design sneakers," "handcrafted Indian footwear," "desi streetwear" drove organic discovery within marketplace search.

Shark Tank moment amplification SMO

The Shark Tank pitch clip was posted to Instagram with full cultural context. Within 48 hours of airing, Instagram follower growth spiked sharply. Bio link updated to D2C store with GA4 event tracking on all new traffic sources.

WhatsApp drops community SME

Core buyers added to a private WhatsApp broadcast. New drops announced here first, giving community members early access. This insider-first approach built genuine loyalty and created organic word-of-mouth before each public launch.

04 — Google Analytics 4  GA4

How GA4 shaped Gully Labs' decisions

D2C funnel tracking

Mapping the content-to-purchase journey

With 35% of revenue already coming from international markets, GA4's geographic audience reports informed where to focus paid acquisition vs organic content. Each Instagram Reel linked with UTM parameters so every sale could be traced to its originating piece of content.

content_view → utm_campaign: 'hockey_drop'
product_page → item: 'gly_001_olive'
add_to_cart → source: 'ig_reel'
purchase → revenue: attributed
repeat_visit → segment: 'loyalist'
Offline expansion logic

GA4 told them where to open stores

Before opening the Panchsheel Enclave flagship and choosing stockists in Bengaluru and Hyderabad, GA4's audience location data showed highest session-to-purchase conversion rates by city. The offline footprint followed the data, not guesswork.

Delhi NCR → highest repeat purchase
Bengaluru → highest avg. order value
Hyderabad → fastest growing sessions
Mumbai → highest referral traffic
International→ 35% of total revenue
Collab attribution

Which collab drove actual sales

Every brand collaboration (Royal Enfield, Tata Sierra, CMF by Nothing) had unique UTM campaign tags. GA4's traffic acquisition report let the team compare press-driven traffic vs creator-driven traffic vs direct search — and double down on what converted.

utm_campaign=royal_enfield_drop
utm_campaign=tata_sierra_collab
utm_campaign=cmf_nothing_launch
utm_campaign=hockey_drop_reel
utm_campaign=shark_tank_episode
Audience insight

Who is buying — and what that changed

GA4 cohort analysis revealed that buyers who entered through cultural story content (Hockey drop, Diwali collection) had significantly higher lifetime value than those from discount-driven channels. This validated the no-discount, story-first model — and justified it to investors at Series A.

Story-first traffic → higher LTV cohort
Discount traffic → lower repeat rate
Mobile sessions → 88% of all traffic
International → 35% revenue share
Top age segment → 22–32 years

07 — What marketers can learn

Key takeaways

01

Build the community before the product  SME

6 months of audience-building before manufacturing began meant Gully Labs launched to a waiting, invested community — not cold traffic. The product was shaped by the people who bought it.

02

Cultural specificity beats generic storytelling  SMO

The Hockey Drop worked because it picked a specific, under-told story. Vague "India pride" content would have been ignored. Specificity created emotional resonance — and shares.

03

Earned backlinks from collabs compound for SEO  SEO

Every brand collaboration generated press coverage. Every press piece was a backlink. Royal Enfield, Tata Sierra, CMF by Nothing — each one added domain authority that money couldn't directly buy.

04

GA4 told them where to open stores  GA4

Offline expansion followed GA4 data — not instinct. Highest repeat-purchase cities got the first physical touchpoints. This is what separates data-led growth from gut-led guessing.

05

UTM every collab, every creator  GA4

With multiple high-profile collabs running simultaneously, UTM tagging in GA4 was the only way to know which partnership drove actual revenue vs just press mentions and impressions.

06

High LTV cohorts justify premium pricing  GA4

GA4 cohort analysis showed story-first customers had higher lifetime value than discount-acquired ones. This data validated the no-discount positioning to investors at Series A — a direct line from analytics to fundraising.
